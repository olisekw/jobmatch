What it does

Scans six boards, once

Profesia.sk and Kariéra.sk for Slovakia, Remote OK, Remotive, Jobicy and Arbeitnow for everything remote. One scheduled crawl serves every account — nobody's signup makes the boards get hit harder.

Reads the posting

Skills, remote or hybrid or on-site, seniority, employment type and salary are pulled out of the ad — from the board's own fields where it has them, and from the text where it does not.

Tracks the applications

Applied → interview → offer → rejected, with a dated note on every move. Marking a job applied on the Jobs page is what puts it in the tracker; nothing is typed twice.

How the score works

Four components, each 0–1, weighted by how much you care about them — and the weights are yours to move. 

score = 100 × Σ(wᵢ · cᵢ) / Σ(wᵢ)

…summed over the components that are known — see below.

Skills
How much of what the ad asks for is on your CV.
Work type
Remote, hybrid or on-site against what you said you want.
Location
Where the job is — and a remote job is everywhere.
Seniority
Junior, medior or senior against where you are.

Missing data is not a penalty

Plenty of ads never say whether the role is hybrid, or what seniority they mean. The sum runs over the components that could be read, so a vague posting is scored on what it did say instead of being pushed down the list for being vague. The interface says so too: an unknown component is a hatched bar, never an empty one.

“Worth a look” is derived, never stored

A job is a match when its score clears your threshold and you have not triaged it yet. Nothing writes that down — move a weight or the threshold and the whole list re-sorts on the spot, with no batch job and nothing thrown away.

How it is built

Flask and Postgres, server-rendered pages and vanilla JavaScript. No front-end framework, and the only heavy dependency is the browser one board needs.

  • Shared crawl, per-user everything else. The job pool is one table every account reads; scores, statuses, settings and applications hang off user_id. It is what keeps a hundred signups from becoming a hundred scrapers.
  • The scanner is its own process. Scans live in a database-backed queue, claimed by a worker container with a conditional update, with live progress and cancel read from the row rather than from memory — so any web process can answer “how is it going?”.
  • Scheduling is a query, not a timer. “Is the last scan older than the interval?” survives a restart, cannot drift and cannot double-fire.
  • A board is one file. Each adapter implements one interface and declares which fields it provides, so a board's own structured data always beats the regex fallback.
  • Deployed as four containers — web, worker, Postgres and Caddy for TLS — with nightly backups that have actually been restored.
